# Privacy Policy

_Last updated: 2026-10-01_

> The English copy of the page the app shows at `/privacy`. Generated by `npm run legal:export` from
> `src/legal/legalContent.ts`, which is the text to edit.

Peerly is team chat in invite-only workspaces. What you write goes directly between the members’ devices (peer to peer), encrypted end to end. Our servers sign you in, let only the people a workspace lists in, introduce members’ devices to each other and, when a workspace’s owner has Premium, keep its encrypted history. This document explains what data that involves and what rights you have.

## 1. Data controller

The data controller is Krystian Pawłow (a natural person operating Peerly), Poland. Privacy contact: privacy@peerly.cc.

## 2. How Peerly works

Each workspace has a secret made on its owner’s device. It travels only inside the invite link’s fragment (the part after #, which browsers never send to a server) and stays on members’ devices. The key that encrypts the workspace’s content comes from that secret, so our servers can neither read the content nor find the workspace from it.

The owner signs the workspace’s list of email addresses with their account key. Only people on that list can enter, whoever holds the link.

Members’ devices connect directly (WebRTC) and pass messages to each other. Each device keeps what it has received and passes it on to members it meets later, so someone who was away catches up from whoever is online. Without Premium, nothing you write is kept on our servers.

## 3. What data is processed

Signing in: you sign in with Google, Microsoft, Apple or your organization’s sign-in (OpenID Connect). Your browser sends us the provider’s identity token once, to verify it. We keep neither the token nor your email address or name on our servers. From the token we derive your account ID (a one-way hash of the provider and your account with it), an internal ID (a keyed hash of the account ID) and a member ID (a keyed hash of your email address), which is how a workspace’s list recognizes you. Your name, email address and photo address from the token stay in your browser, to show who is signed in.

Devices and sessions: your device makes a key of its own, which cannot leave the browser; we keep its public half, your sessions (which device, until when) and the cookie that carries a session. When you link devices, we keep each device’s name (its browser and system, which you can change), when it was linked and last active, its certificate, and your account’s keys sealed for each linked device, which we cannot open.

Entering a workspace: when you open a workspace, your device sends the owner-signed list of members’ email addresses. Our Worker checks the signature, derives each member ID and lets you in if yours is among them. The workspace’s channel keeps only those member IDs and a routing ID derived from the workspace’s secret (not the secret); the email addresses are not kept.

While you are online: the workspace’s channel (a Cloudflare Durable Object) knows which account IDs and devices are connected, and passes on encrypted messages, names, typing notices and the set-up of direct connections between members’ devices. It cannot read them.

Content: messages, edits, deletions, reactions, channel names and the workspace’s name are signed by the device that wrote them and encrypted. They go directly to the members’ devices. If the workspace’s owner has Premium, its channel also keeps encrypted copies of the latest 5,000 changes, so members get them when they come back; this history ends when the owner’s Premium does.

Files: a file you attach is kept on your device and goes directly to the members who open it; its name, type, size and, for an image, a small preview travel inside the encrypted message. Members’ devices keep the files they open and pass them to other members who ask. If the workspace’s owner has Premium, your device also seals each file with the workspace’s key and stores it in Cloudflare R2 under a name derived from the key, so members can get it while nobody who has it is online. We cannot open these files or tell which file one is; we see their size and when they were stored. Each workspace may keep up to 5 GB, and the files are deleted when the owner’s Premium ends.

Your name in a workspace: the name you signed in with is sent, encrypted, to the members you meet. Their devices remember the names they have seen in that workspace, and may pass them to other members who have not seen you.

IP address: to connect directly, members’ devices exchange their network addresses (ICE candidates) through the workspace’s channel, including the public address Cloudflare’s STUN server finds. The other members of your workspaces can therefore learn your IP address, and from it your approximate location and internet provider. A Premium account’s connections may go through Cloudflare’s TURN relay instead; Cloudflare then sees your IP address and the connection’s metadata, and the other members see the relay’s address. We pass these addresses only to members of the same workspace and do not keep them.

Your plan: we keep your account ID, when it first and last signed in (updated at most hourly), its plan, when the plan ends, who on our team granted it and when, and an optional note. Our team finds an account by the account ID shown in Settings, which you give them when you ask about your plan.

Diagnostics: for each request the service handles, we record which operation ran, its result, how long it took, and the app version. When something fails, we also keep the error message, where in the code it happened, and the service’s log lines for that request; these records hold no IP addresses, request contents or messages. Separately, Cloudflare’s own logs of our service keep each request’s technical details, including your IP address and browser identification (user agent), for 7 days, for security and troubleshooting.

Error reports: if the app fails in your browser, it sends us the error message and where in the app’s code it happened, which page it was on, the app version, and your browser’s name and major version, operating system and device type. Email addresses, links’ query strings and long identifiers are removed first. It holds no IP address, cookie or identifier.

Page views: each page of the app sends an anonymous view: which page it was (never a workspace, channel or account), the site or campaign that sent you, whether the view comes from a signed-in account, and whether the device is a phone, tablet or computer. The service adds the country, region and city Cloudflare estimates from your connection. Views carry no IP address, cookie or identifier, so they cannot be linked to each other or to you; we count them only in total.

On your device: your workspaces (their secrets and member lists), the messages, changes and files your device holds, the names it has seen, the device’s key and, once linked, your account’s keys (in IndexedDB); who is signed in, your theme, language and clock choices (in local storage); unsent drafts (for the tab only); and the app’s own files, so it opens offline (in the service worker’s cache).

> No advertising, no tracking pixels, no visitor IDs, no profiling. We do not sell data.

## 4. Legal bases (GDPR Art. 6)

- Performing the service you ask for (Art. 6(1)(b)): signing in, checking a workspace’s list, connecting members’ devices, a Premium workspace’s history and relays, your plan.
- Legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)): security, preventing abuse, diagnostics and error reports to keep the service working, and counting page views in total.
- Workspace owners decide whose email addresses their lists hold, and need their own basis for inviting those people.

## 5. Who can see your data

- Members of your workspaces: your name, what you write there, when you are online, and your IP address when your devices connect directly. Members see the workspace’s list of email addresses.
- Cloudflare, which hosts the service: encrypted content, account and device IDs, connection metadata and IP addresses (request logs, STUN, TURN); not the keys to the content.
- Our team, in CodeFusion Console (the operator’s administration tool, also on Cloudflare): your account ID, when you were first and last seen and your plan; diagnostics and error reports.
- The provider you sign in with, which knows you signed in to Peerly.

## 6. Processors

- Cloudflare Workers and Durable Objects: sign-in, sessions, devices, workspace channels, encrypted history, plans.
- Cloudflare R2: a Premium workspace’s sealed files.
- Cloudflare STUN and TURN: finding a device’s public address for direct connections, and relaying a Premium account’s connections.
- Cloudflare Static Assets: serving the app’s files.
- Cloudflare Workers Analytics Engine and D1, through CodeFusion Console: diagnostics, error reports and page view counts.
- Identity providers (Google, Microsoft, Apple, or your organization’s): signing in.

Cloudflare may process data outside the European Economic Area under the EU Standard Contractual Clauses and the EU–US Data Privacy Framework.

## 7. Retention

- On your devices: until you leave the workspace, clear messages in Settings → Storage, or clear the browser’s data. Signing out keeps them for when you sign in again.
- Sessions: until they expire (30 days without use) or you sign out. Linked devices: until you unlink them.
- A workspace’s member IDs: while the workspace is used; a newer list replaces them.
- A Premium workspace’s history: the latest 5,000 changes, and its sealed files, while the owner’s Premium lasts; both are deleted when Premium ends.
- Your account ID, devices and plan: until you ask us to delete your account.
- Error reports: 30 days; request, diagnostics and page view statistics: 3 months; Cloudflare’s request logs: 7 days.

## 8. Your rights

You may ask for access to your data, its correction or deletion, restriction of processing and a copy (portability), and object to processing based on legitimate interests. Write to privacy@peerly.cc with your account ID from Settings: we keep no email address to find you by. You may also lodge a complaint with the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (UODO), Poland.

We cannot delete copies of your messages that other members’ devices already hold; ask the workspace’s members, or leave and ask its owner to remove you from the list.

## 9. Cookies and local storage

Peerly sets one cookie, which carries your session to our servers, is readable only by them and lasts 30 minutes at a time. Local storage and IndexedDB hold what section 3 lists. All of it is needed for the service you asked for; there are no analytics or advertising cookies, so we do not ask for consent.

## 10. Age

Peerly is for people aged 16 or over.

## 11. Security

Content is encrypted end to end with keys our servers never receive, and every change is signed by the device that made it; a device’s key cannot be copied out of its browser. Traffic to our servers uses HTTPS. Keep your devices locked and approve new devices only when they are yours: anyone with your unlocked device can read your workspaces.

## 12. Changes

We will update this policy when Peerly changes what it processes, and show the date of the latest update at the top.
